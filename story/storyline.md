# 55 Spring Festivals

In 1972, fourteen-year-old Wei Lam leaves Meizhou, in the Hakka heartland of Guangdong, with one small cardboard suitcase tied with string. He has come to a village in Mauritius to help his Uncle Lam run a little basalt-stone shop among the sugar-cane fields. He is homesick and cannot speak the language, until a girl from across the road, Smriti, throws him a cricket ball and tells him to just run.

Every Spring Festival, Smriti's father takes a photograph at the shop door. Photo No. 1 shows a stiff uncle and a nervous boy. When Uncle Lam hands Wei the keys in 1979, he gives him one piece of advice: "Take care of this street, and it will take care of you."

The years turn. Wei marries Smriti in a backyard full of neighbours. They argue happily over dumplings and dholl puri, carry their children to the lion dance in Port Louis Chinatown, and fill the shop with laughter. Photo No. 18 is crowded with neighbours nobody invited. They simply came, every year, and became family.

In 2008 a phone call takes Wei back to Meizhou, where his mother is gravely ill. After thirty-six years he is a stranger in his own village, and he no longer knows where he belongs. His mother tells him, "Home is where your children laugh." He flies back to find the shop lit up under a banner reading WELCOME HOME: Smriti and the neighbours kept it running while he was away. Uncle Lam's words have come true.

At the 55th Spring Festival, in 2027, the shop has been restored with its original basalt walls on show. Families from every community share one long table, cousins in Meizhou join by video call, and Photo No. 55 needs a wider lens. Then Wei's granddaughter Mira wins a scholarship to study in China. At the airport she carries the same string-tied suitcase, crossing the ocean in the other direction. Fifty-five years, two homes, one family.

55 Spring Festivals is a work of fiction inspired by the Sino-Mauritian village shops that became the heart of their neighbourhoods. Through one ordinary family, a growing frangipani tree and a photograph taken every New Year, it follows the 55-year journey of friendship between the people of China and Mauritius.

**AI and other tools used.** Claude (Anthropic), used through Claude Code, worked with us as co-writer and art director. It helped develop our story and script, wrote every image prompt and our Python production tools, and visually checked each generated image against the character sheets, sending back any panel where a face, age or detail had drifted. All artwork was generated with Muse Image 1.0 (Meta) through its API. To keep the characters consistent across 55 years, we first generated a reference sheet for each character, then created every older version by editing the previous one (Wei at 14, 21, 26, 32, 50 and 69). The shop was painted once in 1972 and then edited forward to 1984, 2008 and 2027 from the same camera position, so the building, the frangipani tree and Pieter Both mountain change with time while the view stays the same. Each panel was composed from these references. Speech bubbles, captions and page layout were added by our own Python script (Pillow), so every word is exact.
